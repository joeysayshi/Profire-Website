How AFSS works

From inspection to Council, step by step.

STEP
01
Step 01 · Engage

Engage an Accredited Fire Safety Practitioner

If your property must submit an Annual Fire Safety Statement, the owner engages a competent, accredited practitioner to oversee the AFSS process.

Step 02 · Inspect

Annual routine inspection

Every fire safety measure on your property's Fire Safety Schedule is tested against AS1851 and the minimum standards of performance. This is your date of assessment.

Step 03 · Report

Photographic report & defect quote

Our Head Certifiers review the results and produce a photographic report and cost-effective defect repair quote, so every defect is reported transparently.

Step 04 · Repair

Defects resolved, under one roof

Defects must be resolved before the AFSS goes to Council. Our technicians handle the repairs, so there's one team from start to finish.

Step 05 · Certify

Certified & submitted within 90 days

We certify Level 1 and Level 2 measures without restriction, with ample time for you to submit your AFSS inside Council's 90-day window and avoid fines.

If your building is required to submit an Annual Fire Safety Statement to Council, the owner must engage a competent Accredited Fire Safety Practitioner to oversee the process. Every property has its own Fire Safety Schedule listing the measures that must be installed and working.

Council allows 90 days from the date of assessment (your annual fire safety inspection). If the AFSS isn't submitted by the due date, Council can issue fines weekly until it is. Our office team works to finish inspections, reporting and repairs with ample time to spare.

Under Australian Standards, fire safety equipment must be thoroughly tested at least once a year. More complex systems such as fire panels, hydrant pumps and sprinkler pumps need monthly testing. Based on your Fire Safety Schedule, we'll advise which routine inspections your property needs.
