Bushfire Season Fire Safety for NSW Buildings: A Practical Checklist

How fire danger ratings, Total Fire Bans and the Bush Fire Danger Period affect NSW strata and commercial buildings, plus a practical bushfire season checklist.

21 November 2026 · Profire Australia

Summer is nearly here, and for many NSW buildings that means bushfire season fire safety needs to be on the agenda alongside the usual end-of-year jobs. You don't have to be on a rural block to be affected. Fire danger ratings, Total Fire Bans and fire permit rules can change what your contractors are allowed to do on site, and a building that backs onto bushland or open space has its own preparation to think about. This guide covers the rules strata managers, building owners and facility managers should know, and a practical checklist for the months ahead.

The Bush Fire Danger Period

The NSW Rural Fire Service (RFS) says the statutory Bush Fire Danger Period runs from 1 October to 31 March, although it may vary due to local conditions. Some areas start earlier, so check the RFS website for your area.

During this period, the RFS says a fire permit is required for burning activities in Rural Fire districts. In Fire and Rescue NSW fire districts, a permit is required all year round. The RFS also notes that a permit is required at all times if a fire is likely to be dangerous to a building. For most strata and commercial sites the simple answer is: don't plan any burning without checking with the fire authority first.

Understanding fire danger ratings

Australia uses four fire danger rating levels. The NSW Government describes them like this:

  • Moderate: most fires can be controlled. Plan and prepare.
  • High: fires can be dangerous. Be ready to act.
  • Extreme: fires will spread quickly and be extremely dangerous. Take action now to protect your life and property.
  • Catastrophic: if a fire starts and takes hold, lives are likely to be lost. For your survival, leave bushfire-risk areas.

On days of minimal risk, such as rainy days, the RFS says you'll see "No Rating" on signs, apps and websites.

You can check the rating for your area on the RFS website, in the Hazards Near Me app, or by calling the Bush Fire Information Line on 1800 679 737. Safe Work Australia notes that ratings are provided for the day and the three days ahead, so you can check before you book work.

Total Fire Bans and hot works on your site

On some days, usually when the rating is Extreme or Catastrophic, the RFS Commissioner may declare a Total Fire Ban. During a ban, the RFS says you cannot light, maintain or use a fire in the open, or carry out any activity in the open that causes, or is likely to cause, a fire.

This is the part building managers most often miss. The RFS says general purpose hot works, such as welding, grinding, gas cutting or any activity that produces a spark or flame, are not to be done in the open during a Total Fire Ban. That can affect rooftop plant repairs, balcony balustrade work, car park works and other outdoor trades. Some activities are covered by exemptions published in the Government Gazette, and the RFS says specific exemptions can be applied for, but they take time to process.

A few practical habits help:

  • Check the fire danger rating and any Total Fire Ban before approving outdoor hot works, and again on the morning of the job.
  • Ask contractors how they'll reschedule if a ban is declared.
  • Remind residents and tenants about the barbecue rules. The RFS says solid-fuel barbecues and wood-fired pizza ovens can't be used outside during a Total Fire Ban, and gas and electric barbecues can only be used under strict conditions, including no combustible material within two metres and a responsible adult in control at all times.

Fire permits are also automatically suspended on Total Fire Ban days.

Get the building and grounds ready

The NSW Government says it's important to prepare your property before every bushfire season, whether you own or rent. Its checklist is written for homes, but many items apply just as well to a strata block or commercial site, especially one near bushland:

  • Clean gutters of debris and repair damaged or missing roof tiles.
  • Keep lawns short and gardens maintained.
  • Prune plants that overhang buildings.
  • Clean up debris and dispose of green waste regularly.
  • Remove flammable items such as leaves, sticks and firewood from around buildings.
  • Store gas bottles as far away from buildings as possible, with the outlet pointing away.
  • Repair or cover gaps in external walls, and fit seals around doors and windows.

For strata schemes, much of this sits with the owners corporation's grounds maintenance contract, so it's worth raising with your gardener or building manager before the hotter months set in.

Make sure your fire safety measures are ready

Bushfire preparation is also a good reminder to check the fire safety measures you already have. Hydrants, hose reels, sprinklers, fire doors and alarm systems all need to be working if a fire ever does reach your building, from any cause.

Before summer, it's sensible to:

  • Confirm your routine AS1851 maintenance is up to date, and that any defects from the last visit have been fixed.
  • Keep hydrant boosters, hose reels and fire pump rooms clear of stored items, vegetation and garden waste.
  • Make sure fire doors close and latch properly and aren't propped open during hot weather.
  • Clear blocked access to fire stairs, exits and fire brigade access points.

Review your emergency plan

Safe Work Australia says businesses should use fire danger ratings and warnings in their risk assessment, and plan control measures for each warning level before any fire. Its guidance includes checking the rating ahead of time, checking again at the start of each day and throughout the day in bushfire season, and making sure outdoor or isolated workers know how to monitor for fires. On Catastrophic days in bushfire areas, it suggests postponing work or relocating workers where possible.

Above all, Safe Work Australia's advice is clear: if a fire starts near you, take action immediately to protect your life and your workers' lives. Do not wait for a warning.

Get ready before the heat arrives

Keep an eye on the ratings, plan outdoor works around Total Fire Bans, keep the grounds tidy and make sure your fire safety measures are ready to work.
