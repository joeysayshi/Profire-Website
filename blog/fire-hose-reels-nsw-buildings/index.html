Fire Hose Reels in NSW Buildings: Who Needs Them and How to Look After Them

Does your NSW building need fire hose reels? What the NCC says, why new apartments often have extinguishers instead, and how hose reels must be maintained.

12 November 2026 · Profire Australia

Most people walk past them every day without a second thought: the red reel of hose in a cabinet, a stairwell lobby or a car park. Fire hose reels in NSW buildings are designed for first-attack firefighting, giving occupants a steady supply of water to tackle a small fire before it grows. But not every building needs them, newer apartment buildings often don't have them at all, and where they are installed they need regular attention. This guide explains the rules and what building owners and strata managers should be doing.

What a fire hose reel is for

A fire hose reel is a length of hose on a fixed reel, connected to the building's water supply, with a nozzle at the end. It's meant to be used by occupants on an ordinary fire involving things like paper, timber, fabric and furniture, while the fire is still small.

It is not the same as a fire hydrant. Hydrants are for the fire brigade, with much higher flows and larger hoses. Hose reels are for building occupants, and they're one of the items that can appear on your building's fire safety schedule.

Does your building need fire hose reels?

For new buildings, the requirement comes from the National Construction Code (NCC). In NCC 2022 it sits in clause E1D3 of Volume One. The ABCB's explanatory notes for that clause describe these key points:

  • When hose reels are required: they're required where internal fire hydrants are provided, and the clause also uses a 500 m² floor area threshold, which the ABCB says represents the level of hazard.
  • Common exemptions: the clause exempts Class 2, 3 and 5 buildings and Class 4 parts of buildings (apartments, hotels and similar accommodation, and offices), as well as Class 9c aged care buildings and the classrooms and associated corridors of primary and secondary schools. School halls, gymnasiums and similar areas that may be used outside school hours still need them.
  • Extinguishers instead: where hose reels aren't required in those buildings, portable fire extinguishers must be installed to cover Class A fire risks under clause E1D14.
  • Coverage and location: hose reels must achieve the coverage required by AS 2441, the Australian Standard for hose reel installation. They can be internal, external or both, and they are not permitted inside a fire-isolated exit.

The NCC's exact clause wording, and how it applies to your specific building, should be checked by a certifier or fire safety engineer. But the general picture is useful: if your apartment building is relatively new, it's quite normal for it to have extinguishers rather than hose reels.

Why many newer apartment buildings don't have them

This change didn't come out of nowhere. In 2013, a regulation impact statement prepared for the Australian Building Codes Board assessed the need for fire hose reels in new residential buildings. Its recommended option was to remove the requirement for hose reels in new Class 2 and Class 3 buildings and Class 4 parts (except in alpine areas) and to require additional ABE fire extinguishers instead.

Older buildings: your fire safety schedule decides

Here's the important part for owners of existing buildings. A change to the NCC for new buildings doesn't mean you can remove the hose reels in an older building.

Every building's essential fire safety measures are listed in its fire safety schedule, along with the standard of performance each one must meet. If fire hose reels are on your schedule, they're an essential fire safety measure. That means they must be maintained, and they need to be covered when your annual fire safety statement is prepared.

Building Commission NSW also notes that AS 1851-2012 is an inspection, testing and maintenance standard, and doesn't itself require system upgrades. If you're thinking about removing or changing a measure on your schedule, get advice from a certifier first rather than simply decommissioning it.

Maintaining hose reels under AS 1851-2012

From 13 February 2026, AS 1851-2012 is mandatory in NSW under the Environmental Planning and Assessment (Development Certification and Fire Safety) Regulation 2021. According to Building Commission NSW:

  • All Class 1b and Class 2 to 9 buildings in NSW must have essential fire safety measures inspected and tested in accordance with AS 1851-2012, unless there is a performance solution in place.
  • This applies to new and existing buildings. For existing buildings, the yearly service is expected to be completed in February 2027.
  • Building owners must keep records in the manner prescribed by AS 1851-2012 for at least 7 years and make them available for inspection.
  • A hard copy logbook must be left on site at the completion of testing, and authorities such as council or Fire and Rescue NSW can ask to see it.

AS 1851 sets out what's checked on hose reels and how often. A qualified technician will inspect, test and record the results, and flag anything that needs repair. Profire's routine fire inspections follow AS1851 and come with a photographic report, so you can see the condition of each reel at a glance.

What owners and strata managers can do between services

You don't need to be a technician to help keep hose reels ready for use. Some simple habits make a real difference:

  • Keep them accessible. Make sure reels aren't blocked by bikes, boxes, bins or stored furniture, especially in car parks and storerooms.
  • Use them only for firefighting. Hose reels shouldn't be used for washing down cars, balconies or bin rooms. Everyday use wears out components and can leave the reel damaged or not properly rewound when it's needed.
  • Report damage straight away. Leaks, kinks, cracked nozzles, a damaged cabinet or a reel that won't turn should be passed on to your fire safety contractor.
  • Check the logbook and service tags. If you can't see recent service records, ask your contractor when the reels were last inspected.
  • Keep your records together. Service reports and logbook entries make the AFSS process much smoother.

If a hose reel is damaged or leaking, our team can arrange fire equipment repairs.

Know when not to use a hose reel

Hose reels deliver water, and water isn't the right choice for every fire. Fire and Rescue NSW warns: never use water to put out a fat or oil fire, because water can cause the fire to spread rapidly and cause serious burns. For a cooking fire, FRNSW advises turning off the stove if it's safe to do so and covering the flame with a lid or a fire blanket.

If a fire is growing, don't try to fight it. Get everyone out, close the door behind you and call Triple Zero (000).
