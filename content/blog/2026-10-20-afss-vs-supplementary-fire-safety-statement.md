Most building owners and strata managers in NSW know the annual fire safety statement (AFSS) well. Fewer have come across its lesser-known sibling, the supplementary fire safety statement. Both are declarations about the fire safety measures in your building, and both use the same NSW Government form, but they cover different measures and follow different timetables. If you're not sure what separates an AFSS vs a supplementary fire safety statement, or whether your building needs both, this guide walks through it.

## Two statements, one set of rules

Both statements are covered by Part 12 of the *Environmental Planning and Assessment (Development Certification and Fire Safety) Regulation 2021*. NSW Planning describes a fire safety statement as a document issued by or on behalf of the owner of an existing building. In each case, the owner is declaring that an accredited practitioner (fire safety) has assessed, inspected and verified the performance of the fire safety measures the statement covers.

What changes between the two is *which* measures are covered and *how often* the statement is due.

## What the AFSS covers

The annual fire safety statement must be issued each year and include **all** the essential fire safety measures that apply to your building. Essential fire safety measures are listed on the building's fire safety schedule. The Department of Planning's fact sheet gives examples of fire safety measures such as portable fire extinguishers, fire hydrants, fire sprinklers, fire detection and alarm systems.

The AFSS also confirms that an accredited practitioner has inspected the building's exit systems and found them compliant with the Regulation.

A few rules worth remembering:

- **Every year.** A building owner must ensure an AFSS is issued each year.
- **A 3-month window.** FPA Australia's guide to the Regulation notes that the assessment and inspection must happen within the 3 months before the statement is issued.
- **Copies to council and Fire and Rescue NSW.** A copy goes to your local council and to the Commissioner of Fire and Rescue NSW (FRNSW), together with the current fire safety schedule.
- **On display.** The statement and the current fire safety schedule must be prominently displayed in the building.

## What the supplementary statement covers

A supplementary fire safety statement deals only with **critical fire safety measures**. According to the NSW Department of Planning's fire safety statements fact sheet, these are measures that "require periodic assessment and inspection at intervals of less than 12 months." They are specifically identified on the fire safety schedule, and every critical fire safety measure is also an essential fire safety measure.

In other words, a critical measure still appears on your AFSS each year, but it also needs its own, more frequent statement in between.

The key points:

- **Only some buildings need them.** The requirements apply to owners of buildings whose fire safety schedule lists critical fire safety measures.
- **The schedule sets the timetable.** The interval for each critical measure (always less than 12 months) is written on the fire safety schedule. FPA Australia's guide explains that the certifier sets these intervals when the schedule is prepared.
- **Same lodgement and display duties.** FRNSW notes that the owner must give FRNSW a copy of each supplementary statement as soon as practicable after it's issued, with the current fire safety schedule, and display it in the building. FPA Australia's guide notes the Regulation also refers to supplementary statements being given to the council.

## AFSS vs supplementary fire safety statement at a glance

- **Measures covered:** the AFSS covers every essential fire safety measure on the schedule, plus the exit systems. The supplementary statement covers only the critical fire safety measures.
- **How often:** the AFSS is due every year. The supplementary statement is due at the intervals set on the schedule, which are always less than 12 months.
- **Which buildings:** every building with essential fire safety measures on its schedule needs an AFSS. Only buildings whose schedule lists critical measures need supplementary statements.
- **Form:** both use the standard form approved by the Planning Secretary. The fact sheet notes the owner signs section 8 of the form for an annual statement and section 9 for a supplementary statement.
- **Copies and display:** both go to the authorities with the current fire safety schedule and must be displayed in the building.

## Rules that apply to both

**The form can't be altered.** The Department's fact sheet says a fire safety statement must be issued in the form approved by the Planning Secretary, and that adding letterheads or logos, or adding or removing sections, may breach the Regulation.

**The owner issues it.** The statement is issued by or on behalf of the owner, which might be an individual, a company or an owners corporation. An agent issuing it on the owners' behalf needs their prior authority.

**The person declaring can't be the assessor.** The person who signs the declaration must not be a practitioner involved in assessing the measures or inspecting the building for that statement, or their employer, employee or direct associate.

**Each practitioner must be identified.** The form must include the details and signatures of each accredited practitioner who assessed the measures, or the owner can attach a separate signed document from each one.

**Missing deadlines has consequences.** Councils such as Randwick note that failing to submit a complete fire safety statement is an offence that can lead to a penalty notice, and that council can't extend the due date.

## How to tell if your building needs supplementary statements

The answer is on your fire safety schedule. Look for any measures marked as critical fire safety measures, along with their required intervals. If there are none, an AFSS each year is all you need. If there are, put each supplementary due date in your calendar alongside your AFSS date. They're easy to forget because they don't line up with the annual cycle.

If you can't find your fire safety schedule, your council or the building's certifier may be able to help, and it's worth getting a copy before your next inspection.

Keeping on top of [routine fire inspections](/routine-fire-inspections/) throughout the year also makes each statement smoother, because the measures have already been tested and any defects picked up early.
