The weeks before Christmas are busy for everyone, and that includes your building's fire safety. Lobbies get decorated, shops fill their storerooms with extra stock, and plenty of businesses close their doors for a week or two. **Holiday season fire safety** is mostly about making sure none of those changes quietly undo the protection your building already has. This guide pulls together Fire and Rescue NSW (FRNSW) advice and a few practical checks for strata managers, building owners and small business operators.

## Why the holidays bring extra fire risk

FRNSW says it responds to approximately 4,500 residential fires each year. Half of them start in the kitchen, mostly from unattended cooking, and electrical appliances and faults cause almost 40% of home fires. Heaters, cigarettes and candles are also common causes.

The festive season adds more of exactly these things: more cooking and entertaining, more lights and power boards, more candles and more people in and out of buildings. In shops and common areas it also adds clutter, which is where fire exits tend to suffer.

## Decorations and lights in common areas

FRNSW's festive season advice applies just as much to a strata foyer or a shopfront as it does to a living room:

- **Use lights with an Australian Standards label** and set them up following the manufacturer's instructions.
- **Check before you hang.** Look for faults, worn plugs and frayed cords. If in doubt, replace the lights.
- **One item per power socket.** Don't overload power boards or piggy-back double adaptors. FRNSW recommends power boards with both overload and earth leakage protection.
- **Keep lights away from flammable materials** such as wrapping paper, decorations and curtains, because decorative lights can get very hot.
- **Take care with artificial trees.** Firefighters warn that synthetic trees can be highly flammable, so use lights designed for them.
- **Switch displays off** when they're not being watched. FRNSW says to turn off electrical displays and decorative lights before bed. For a common area or shop, a timer that switches them off after hours does the same job.
- **Skip the candles** in shared spaces, or never leave them burning unattended.

Before anything goes up, take a moment to look at where it's going. Decorations shouldn't hide or block exit signs, fire hose reels, extinguishers, hydrants or the fire indicator panel. Firefighters and occupants need to find these quickly in an emergency.

## Keep fire exits and fire doors clear

This is the one that matters most. The NSW Government's "Offence relating to fire exits" notice, which you'll see displayed near many fire exits, states that it's an offence under the *Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979* to:

- place anything in or near a fire exit that may obstruct people moving to and from the exit
- interfere with, obstruct or impede the operation of any fire doors.

Over the holidays the usual culprits are delivery cartons and extra stock stacked in corridors, Christmas trees and displays placed near exit doors, bikes and furniture left in stairwells during move-outs, and fire doors wedged open to make deliveries easier. A fire door that's propped open can't stop smoke and fire spreading through the building.

![Technician testing an illuminated green exit sign](svc9 "Exit signs should stay visible and unobstructed, decorations included")

A quick walk-through before your busiest week is worth it. Check that every exit route is clear from start to finish, that exit signs are lit and visible, and that fire doors close and latch on their own.

## Before you close for the break

If your business or building is closing or running with reduced staff over Christmas, the fire safety measures still have to work while nobody is there. Before you lock up:

1. **Report and fix outstanding faults.** If your fire panel is showing a fault or has been isolated, don't leave it that way over the shutdown. Get it sorted before you go.
2. **Make sure you're not overdue for servicing.** If routine testing is due in December or January, book it early. Our [routine fire inspections](/routine-fire-inspections/) follow AS1851 and come with a photographic report.
3. **Switch off non-essential electrical items,** including decorative displays, and unplug devices that don't need to stay on.
4. **Don't leave devices charging.** FRNSW warns against leaving lithium-ion devices charging when you leave home or go to sleep, against keeping them constantly on charge, and against storing or charging them near exit points.
5. **Check your contacts.** Make sure your alarm monitoring company, building manager and fire service provider have current after-hours contact details for the break.

## Smoke alarms and residents

For strata buildings, the holidays are a good time to remind residents about the basics. FRNSW says the risk of dying in a home fire is halved when a working smoke alarm is present. Its advice is to test alarms, replace faulty batteries or alarms, and keep in mind that smoke alarms have a lifespan of ten years. The legal minimum in NSW homes is at least one working smoke alarm on each level.

FRNSW also recommends that every household has an escape plan with two safe ways out of every room, and that people never go back inside a burning building. A short note in the lift or a strata newsletter in early December costs nothing. If your building's alarms are due for attention, our [smoke alarm testing](/smoke-alarm-testing/) service can help.
