Summer means barbecues on balconies, rooftop terraces and common-property BBQ areas in apartment buildings across NSW. In a building full of neighbours, a small mistake with an open flame doesn't stay small for long. **Balcony BBQ safety** comes down to a few habits: an in-date cylinder in good condition, clear space around the flame, and safe storage afterwards. This guide pulls together advice from Fire and Rescue NSW (FRNSW), NSW Fair Trading and Homes NSW.

## Why balcony barbecues need extra care

On a house, a barbecue usually sits in an open backyard. On a balcony it sits close to walls, glass, furniture, plants and the apartments above and beside it. FRNSW's high-rise fire safety advice is direct: make sure BBQs and heaters on balconies are **at least a metre away from all objects**, and turn them off when they're not in use.

FRNSW also says to only use a barbecue in a well-ventilated area, because the fumes and gases it gives off can be harmful. Never use portable LPG cylinders indoors or in confined spaces, and never use outdoor heating or cooking equipment inside the home, because carbon monoxide can build up and could be fatal. An enclosed or glassed-in balcony isn't the open air.

## Before the first BBQ of summer: check the cylinder

LPG cylinders have to be periodically tested. According to NSW Fair Trading, a cylinder must have a **valid 10-year test stamp** before it can be refilled, and you're not allowed to refill a cylinder yourself. Homes NSW's advice is to re-test and stamp gas cylinders every 10 years.

Before you connect the cylinder, run through this quick check:

1. **Find the test stamp.** Look for a current, legible test date. Fair Trading says not to use out-of-date cylinders. If you're buying a cylinder, ask the retailer to show you the test mark.
2. **Look for damage.** Don't use a cylinder that's dented, rusted or corroded. Homes NSW says these must not be refilled and need to go to a test station for re-certification.
3. **Check the hose and connections.** FRNSW says never to use a hose that has perished or is cracked, and to make sure connections are clean, tight and fit snugly before you light up.
4. **Do the soapy water test.** Brush or spray soapy water onto the hose and connections and watch for bubbles. **Never check for leaks with a naked flame.** If you're unsure, turn the gas off and have a licensed gas fitter check the hose and connection.
5. **Follow the manufacturer's instructions** for start-up, shut-down and maintenance. FRNSW also recommends having the barbecue serviced, including checking hoses and connections for blockages or perishing.

## Using a barbecue on a balcony safely

Once the equipment checks out, FRNSW's day-to-day advice is simple:

- **Keep a metre of clear space** around the barbecue on every side, including from walls, furniture, umbrellas, plants and washing.
- **Never leave it unattended.** There should be an adult in charge of a lit barbecue at all times, and children kept well away.
- **Never put flammable liquids on a barbecue.**
- **Check the fire danger rating first.** FRNSW says to check your local fire danger rating before lighting up, because there may be a Total Fire Ban.
- **Mind the wind.** Homes NSW advises against using LPG appliances in winds over 10 km/h, and FRNSW suggests sheltering the barbecue from wind gusts.
- **Have something on hand.** FRNSW says a fire extinguisher nearby is worth having, along with a water supply.
- **Turn the gas off at the cylinder** when you've finished, not just at the burner knobs.

Smoke drifting back inside can also set off detectors, and in buildings with a monitored fire alarm that can bring a fire truck. Close the balcony door while you cook, and see our guide to [false fire alarm charges in NSW](/blog/false-fire-alarm-charges-nsw/).

## Storing gas cylinders in an apartment building

Storage is where a lot of apartment-building risks hide. Homes NSW's advice is to store cylinders outdoors in a well-ventilated area, **upright at all times**, with the valve turned off and the pressure relief valve pointed away from buildings and anything combustible. Keep them away from petrol, aerosols and other flammable items. Fair Trading adds: keep cylinders away from heat and direct sunlight.

In a strata building, that rules out a few places people often use:

- **Inside the apartment,** including laundries and storage cupboards.
- **Fire stairs and exit corridors.** FRNSW says never to leave anything in fire stairs or prop open fire doors. A gas cylinder in an escape route is the last thing anyone wants there in a fire.
- **Enclosed storage cages or basement car parks** without good ventilation. Check your by-laws and ask your strata manager where cylinders should be kept.

Old or unwanted cylinders can go to an approved gas cylinder test station, some council recycling centres or some gas suppliers. Don't put them in the bin.

## If something goes wrong

If you smell gas around the barbecue, FRNSW says to shut off the cylinder immediately and let the gas dissipate. Homes NSW says that if you detect a strong smell of gas, call **Triple Zero (000)** and ask for the fire service.

If there's a fire you can't safely control, get out, stay out and call Triple Zero (000). In an apartment building, FRNSW's advice is to use the fire stairs, not the lifts, and never go back inside a burning building. Residents should know where the fire exits and firefighting equipment on their floor are before they ever need them.

## What strata managers and owners corporations can do

NSW Fair Trading explains that by-laws are rules all residents in a strata scheme must follow, and that balcony by-laws can cover what you can put on the balcony. Rules on barbecues vary from scheme to scheme, so check yours. If a resident keeps breaching a by-law, the steps are a warning, a notice to comply, free mediation through Fair Trading, and then the NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal, which can issue fines.

Practical steps for the start of summer:

- **Remind residents** in a newsletter or lift notice: one metre of clearance, the soapy water test, cylinders stored outdoors and upright, and nothing in the fire stairs.
- **Check common-property BBQ areas.** Make sure the equipment is maintained and that the nearest extinguisher is in place and in date. If anything needs attention, our [fire equipment repairs](/fire-equipment-repairs/) team can help.
