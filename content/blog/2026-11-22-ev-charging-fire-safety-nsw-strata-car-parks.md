More apartment residents are driving electric vehicles, and NSW strata law is changing to make it easier for them to charge at home. For strata committees, building managers and owners, that raises a fair question: what does **EV charging fire safety** look like in a shared basement car park? This guide sums up what the law change does, what Fire and Rescue NSW (FRNSW) recommends, and the practical steps that help keep your building's fire safety measures working as chargers go in.

## The new "right to charge" for NSW strata owners

The NSW Government's guide to strata law changes lists "establishing a right for owners to install electric vehicle ('EV') charging infrastructure on their lot" among the reforms **planned to start in 2027**. The change is part of the Strata Schemes Legislation Amendment (Miscellaneous) Bill 2025, which was reported as passing NSW Parliament in October 2026.

As reported, the reforms mean a by-law that unreasonably prohibits a charging station on a lot will have no effect. The owner gives the owners corporation notice of the installation, the owners corporation can object in writing with reasons (but not unreasonably), and the owner pays for the installation. The exact start date and the finer details are still to be confirmed, so check the NSW Government's strata pages before relying on them.

What the law change doesn't do is replace any of your building's existing fire safety obligations. Every essential fire safety measure on your fire safety schedule still needs to be maintained and certified in your annual fire safety statement, chargers or not.

## What Fire and Rescue NSW recommends for EV charging in buildings

In March 2025, FRNSW published a fire safety position paper on electric vehicles and EV charging equipment in the built environment. It endorses guidance from AFAC (the national council for fire and emergency services) for practitioners who design and certify Class 2 to 9 buildings with EV parking or charging. It's guidance rather than a new law, and FRNSW notes its position will evolve.

The key recommendations are:

- **Charge outside where possible.** EV charging should be located externally or in open air where possible.
- **Sprinkler protection indoors.** Where charging is inside a car park, the car park should be protected by an automatic fire sprinkler system with performance equivalent to a system complying with AS 2118.1 or AS 2118.6.
- **Hydrant coverage.** The car park should also be protected by fire hydrant coverage.

The paper also points to the National Construction Code. Since 1 May 2023, NCC 2022 provision J9D4 has required dedicated electrical infrastructure for EV charging in the car parks of certain new buildings.

For an existing strata building, this means a sprinkler-protected basement with good hydrant coverage is in a much better position to host chargers than one without. If you're not sure what your car park has, your fire safety schedule lists it.

## Practical steps for strata committees and building managers

Here's a sensible checklist to work through as residents start asking about chargers.

1. **Check your fire safety schedule.** Confirm whether the car park has sprinklers, hydrants, hose reels, smoke detection or mechanical ventilation, and that each is listed on the schedule.
2. **Keep fire equipment clear.** Chargers, cabling and bollards shouldn't block access to hydrants, hose reels, extinguishers, exit doors or exit signs.
3. **Protect fire-rated walls and slabs.** New cabling that passes through fire-rated walls or floors needs properly sealed penetrations. Unsealed holes are a common defect after electrical work. Our guide to [passive fire protection](/blog/passive-fire-protection-penetrations-collars/) explains why.
4. **Use qualified people.** Have a licensed electrician carry out charger installations, and keep the paperwork with your building records.
5. **Tell your fire safety contractor.** Let your contractor know when chargers are added so they can look at the affected area during [routine fire inspections](/routine-fire-inspections/) and the next [AFSS inspection](/afss-inspections/).
6. **Update your emergency information.** Make sure building managers and residents know where chargers are located, and that the information given to responders is current.

## Day-to-day EV safety advice from FRNSW

FRNSW's advice for EV owners is worth sharing with residents through your building newsletter or noticeboard:

- **Don't charge a damaged EV.** FRNSW says not to charge an EV that has been subject to any suspected damage, such as a collision, road debris or flood immersion.
- **Damaged batteries can ignite later.** FRNSW warns that damaged EV batteries may ignite hours, days or even weeks after the initial incident. A damaged EV should be kept in an open area at least 15 metres from other vehicles and buildings, and not charged or used until it has been inspected and cleared by a qualified technician.
- **Look for the blue "EV" badge.** EVs should carry a blue "EV" badge on the number plate so responders know there's a high-voltage battery.
- **Smoke or heat alarms in private garages.** FRNSW recommends a smoke alarm or heat alarm in Class 10a buildings (private garages) where an EV is regularly parked or charged, ideally interconnected mains-powered devices installed by a licensed electrician.

## If an EV catches fire in your car park

FRNSW's advice is clear: apply the park brake and turn the vehicle off if it's safe to do so, then get out and stay at least **30 metres** away, keeping others back too. Call **Triple Zero (000)** and tell the operator the vehicle involved is an EV. Make sure remote keys can't accidentally start the vehicle.

Residents shouldn't try to fight a vehicle fire themselves. Your building's job is to make sure sprinklers, hydrants, detection, exits and warning systems are working when the fire brigade arrives, which is exactly what regular maintenance to AS1851 is for.
