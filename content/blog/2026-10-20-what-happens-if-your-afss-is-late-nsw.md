If you own or manage a building in NSW, your annual fire safety statement (AFSS) is one of those dates that quietly comes around every year. Miss it, and it stops being quiet. So what happens if your AFSS is late in NSW? In short: council can issue penalty notices that grow week by week, and the statement is still owed on top of any fine. Here's how it works and how to stay ahead of it.

## A quick refresher: what the AFSS is

An annual fire safety statement is issued by or on behalf of the building's owner. It confirms that an accredited practitioner (fire safety) has assessed, inspected and verified the performance of each essential fire safety measure listed in the building's fire safety schedule, and has checked that the exit systems comply with the Regulation. The rules sit in Part 12 of the *Environmental Planning and Assessment (Development Certification and Fire Safety) Regulation 2021*, and the statement must use the NSW Government's fire safety statement template.

Councils describe the key timing rules like this:

- **Every 12 months.** The statement is due within 12 months of the previous fire safety statement or fire safety certificate.
- **A 3-month window.** The inspections and assessment must be carried out within the 3 months before the statement is issued.
- **Two copies go out.** A copy goes to your local council and another to the Commissioner of Fire and Rescue NSW.
- **On display.** A copy must be displayed in a prominent position in the building.

Some buildings also have critical fire safety measures that need supplementary statements more often. Those intervals are set out in the building's fire safety schedule.

## What happens if your AFSS is late

Not providing an annual fire safety statement by the due date is an offence, and the legal responsibility sits with the owner. Many councils send a courtesy reminder, but they're clear that the reminder doesn't shift that responsibility.

### Weekly penalty notices

The penalty notice amounts for late statements are set in Schedule 5 of the 2021 Regulation. Councils including Burwood, Wollongong and Hornsby publish the same scale:

- First week after the due date: **$1,000**
- Second week: **$2,000**
- Third week: **$3,000**
- Fourth and any later week: **$4,000**

Councils describe these as "substantial and continuing weekly penalty notices", so the longer a statement stays overdue, the more it can cost. Wollongong City Council also notes that amounts can change, so check your own council's page for the current figures.

### The statement is still owed

Paying a fine doesn't close the matter. As Hornsby Shire Council puts it, even after a penalty notice has been issued, an AFSS is still required to be submitted.

### Fire safety orders and court

Councils have other options too. Wollongong City Council notes that a fire safety order may be issued if the statement hasn't been submitted on time. Before an order is issued, council sends the owner a notice of intention, and owners generally have 21 days to respond. Several councils also warn that failing to submit an AFSS could lead to proceedings in the Land and Environment Court.

## Why statements end up late

A late AFSS often comes down to timing rather than neglect. The inspection gets booked close to the due date, and then a defect turns up that has to be repaired and re-tested before the statement can be signed. Or a change of strata manager or building manager means the due date slips through the cracks.

Because the assessment has to happen within the 3 months before the statement is issued, there's a real window to work with. Using it well is the simplest way to avoid trouble.

## How to keep your AFSS on time

1. **Know your due date.** It's 12 months from your last statement or certificate. Some councils, such as Hornsby, offer an online lookup, and your last statement will show the date.
2. **Book the inspection early in the 3-month window.** That leaves room to repair anything that fails and still lodge on time.
3. **Keep up routine maintenance through the year.** Regular servicing to AS1851 means fewer surprises at AFSS time. Our [routine fire inspections](/routine-fire-inspections/) are built around this.
4. **Fix defects promptly.** A failed item can hold up the whole statement. Our [fire equipment repairs](/fire-equipment-repairs/) team can help close them out.
5. **Lodge both copies and display one.** Council and Fire and Rescue NSW each need a copy, and one goes up in the building.
6. **Hand it over properly.** If your building is changing managers, make sure the AFSS due date is part of the handover.

## If you can't make the deadline

Talk to your council before the due date, not after it. Policies vary. Hornsby Shire Council says owners can apply for an extension, which carries an administration fee. Georges River Council will consider a stay of a penalty notice where a statement can't be lodged because of legitimate, unanticipated circumstances, but asks for the application at least 2 weeks before the due date. Check what your own council offers.
