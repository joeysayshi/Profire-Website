When you run a café, a shop or a small office, fire safety can feel like one more job on a long list. But it matters: Small Business NSW points out that small businesses are legally required to make their premises safe, and a fire can disrupt trade and damage your reputation long after the smoke clears. This **fire safety checklist for cafés, shops and small businesses** pulls together the advice from Fire and Rescue NSW (FRNSW), SafeWork NSW and Small Business NSW, so you can work through it one item at a time.

## Start with your lease

Before anything else, work out who is responsible for what. Small Business NSW recommends checking your lease for the roles and responsibilities around fire safety compliance, equipment and maintenance.

That's important because, in most commercial buildings, two sets of obligations sit side by side:

- **The building owner** must maintain the building's essential fire safety measures. Under clause 81 of the *Environmental Planning and Assessment (Development Certification and Fire Safety) Regulation 2021*, each measure has to be kept to the standard of performance in the building's fire safety schedule. Every year the owner must also give council and FRNSW an [annual fire safety statement](/afss-inspections/) (AFSS) and display it in a prominent position in the building.
- **You, as the business,** have a duty of care to the workers and visitors on your premises. FRNSW's small business checklist starts from exactly that point.

If you're not sure which items in your shop are covered by the building's AFSS, ask your landlord or building manager.

## Your small business fire safety checklist

### 1. Have a written emergency plan

SafeWork NSW says it is a legal requirement for **all workplaces** to have an emergency plan. It's a written set of instructions for what workers and others should do in an emergency, and it must include:

- emergency procedures, including an effective response, evacuation procedures, notifying emergency services at the earliest opportunity, medical treatment and assistance, and clear communication between the person coordinating the response and everyone at the workplace
- frequent testing of those procedures
- information, training and instruction for the workers who need to carry them out.

Keep the plan, or a summary of its key points, where workers can easily get to it or on display. Review it regularly so it stays current.

### 2. Practise evacuating

FRNSW recommends displaying your evacuation plan, including where people should assemble, and practising it with your workers **every six months**. SafeWork NSW adds that training should include all workers, including shift workers, part-timers and casuals, and should consider short-term contractors and visitors.

In a shopping centre, arcade or multi-tenanted building, SafeWork NSW says you need to consult with the other businesses when preparing your plan. FRNSW suggests making sure, as part of your lease, that the building manager runs practice evacuations for all tenants.

### 3. Keep exits clear and unlocked

It sounds obvious, but stock deliveries, bins and furniture have a habit of creeping into exit paths. Small Business NSW says emergency exits should be clearly marked, unobstructed and unlocked during business hours.

### 4. Get the right extinguishers, and know how to use them

FRNSW's advice on portable fire extinguishers is practical:

- Put them in a conspicuous, readily accessible spot, with a clearance of at least **1,000 mm** around each one.
- Match the equipment to your fire risks. FRNSW gives the example of foam or dry powder extinguishers for flammable liquid fires, and carbon dioxide extinguishers for electrical fires.
- Install signs so people can find the equipment quickly and see what type of fire it can be used on.
- Train the workers who may need to use it, so they know which extinguisher suits which fire.
- Make sure the equipment is regularly maintained.

Not sure which extinguisher types suit your premises? A fire safety technician can check what you have against the risks in your space during a [routine fire inspection](/routine-fire-inspections/).

### 5. Make sure your alarm system works

FRNSW says your business should have an adequately serviced and functional fire alarm system that suits your business, such as a remotely serviced alarm system, sprinklers, or thermal or smoke alarms. Small Business NSW adds that alarms must be installed and operable, and regularly tested and maintained.

### 6. Look after electrical equipment and machinery

Coffee machines, display fridges, ovens, heaters and power boards all work hard in a small business. FRNSW recommends:

- a regular electrical inspection and maintenance program
- not overloading power points and power boards
- checking electrical equipment regularly for damage and faults
- servicing machinery as the manufacturer recommends and keeping it clean
- switching machinery off when the business is unattended, where possible.

FRNSW also points businesses to SafeWork NSW guidance on charging and storing lithium-ion battery-powered devices safely at work.

### 7. Watch your rubbish and storage

Cardboard boxes and packaging pile up quickly, especially out the back of a shop or café. FRNSW recommends not storing or stockpiling flammable materials such as packaging or waste where the public can get to them, including areas just outside your premises. If you store dangerous goods, their storage and use must meet legislative requirements, and SafeWork NSW notes that workplaces storing dangerous goods may need to lodge an emergency plan with FRNSW.

### 8. Plan for getting back to business

A fire doesn't have to destroy the whole building to close your doors. FRNSW recommends having a Business Continuity Plan, making sure you have sufficient fire insurance, and keeping contingency plans to protect or restore important information such as supplier and client lists, contracts and insurance details.

## Extra tips for cafés and food businesses

If you run a café or restaurant, FRNSW publishes a separate [restaurant fire safety checklist](https://www.fire.nsw.gov.au/fire-safety/workplace-fire-safety/topics/restaurant-fire-safety) to use alongside your Business Continuity Plan. It's worth reading with your kitchen team, because commercial kitchens bring their own fire risks.

## Quick checklist

- Lease checked: you know what the landlord maintains and what you maintain
- Written emergency plan in place, on display and reviewed regularly
- Evacuation practised with all staff in the last six months
- Exits marked, clear and unlocked during business hours
- Extinguishers suited to your risks, signposted, clear of clutter and maintained
- Staff trained to use fire equipment
- Fire alarm system working, tested and maintained
- Electrical equipment and machinery inspected and serviced
- Packaging, waste and flammable materials stored away from public areas
- Business Continuity Plan and fire insurance up to date
