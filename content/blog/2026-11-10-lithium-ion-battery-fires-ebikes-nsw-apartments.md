E-bikes and e-scooters are now part of everyday life in NSW apartment buildings. They're parked in hallways, wheeled into lifts and charged in units, storage cages and car parks. They're also behind a growing number of fires. This guide covers **lithium-ion battery fires in NSW apartment buildings**: what the official figures show, what the new NSW rules cover, and the practical steps strata managers, building owners and facility managers can take.

## How common are lithium-ion battery fires in NSW?

Fire and Rescue NSW (FRNSW) has been warning about these fires for several years. In February 2025, FRNSW Commissioner Jeremy Fewtrell said there were "at least 318 lithium-ion battery fires in NSW" in 2024, up from 272 in 2023 and 165 in 2022.

E-bikes are a big part of the picture. In late November 2025, FRNSW reported it had responded to its **100th lithium-ion battery fire involving an e-bike that year**. That fire started in the front room of a Redfern terrace at around 4am, and two neighbouring terraces had to be evacuated.

FRNSW has also pointed out how fast these fires can cut off escape routes. Describing a run of fires in early 2025, the Commissioner said that in at least three of them, residents were trapped by the flames and had to flee through windows.

## Why these batteries are a fire risk

The NSW Government explains that if a lithium-ion battery overheats uncontrollably, it can release flammable and toxic gases in a process called **thermal runaway**. The risk is higher in batteries that are damaged, overcharged or exposed to high temperatures.

It isn't only e-bikes and e-scooters. NSW Government ministers have noted that everyday items like power tools, vacuum cleaners, portable chargers, electric toothbrushes and shavers can also be involved.

For apartment buildings, the concern is simple: a fire that starts in one unit, storage cage or basement can affect everyone who shares the building and its exits.

## What the new NSW rules cover

NSW has introduced mandatory standards for e-micromobility products (e-bikes, e-scooters and similar devices):

- **Safety standards, from 1 February 2025.** Retailers and manufacturers can only sell e-micromobility devices in NSW with components that comply with the prescribed safety standards. The first stage covers the vehicles, their batteries and their chargers. The government announced later stages for mandatory testing and certification, and for labelling.
- **Information standard, from 19 February 2025.** Sellers must give buyers information on product safety, fire safety and emergency procedures, storage and end of life, electrical safety, and road rules. It was updated in June 2026.

These rules apply to the people who **sell** the products. They don't regulate how residents charge or store devices in your building. That part is still up to owners, strata committees and residents, so it pays to plan for it.

## FRNSW's advice for charging and storing batteries

FRNSW's published safety advice is a good basis for a building notice or a residents' newsletter. It includes:

- Never charge batteries overnight or while you're asleep, and never leave home with devices charging.
- Don't leave devices constantly on charge.
- Always use the correct charger for your device, and never "mix and match" components.
- Don't modify batteries or devices to increase speed or range.
- Don't charge devices on beds, sofas or near highly flammable materials.
- Try to charge devices outside if possible.
- Don't store or charge lithium-ion devices near exit points.
- Store batteries and devices in a cool, dry area, away from combustible materials.
- Buy from reputable retailers and avoid cheap, substandard devices.
- Don't put lithium-ion batteries in the rubbish. Take damaged or end-of-life batteries to a battery recycling facility, or ask your council.

The NSW Government also suggests checking the ACCC Product Safety website to see whether a product has an active recall.

## Practical steps for strata and building managers

You can't control every device in every unit, but you can reduce the risk in the parts of the building you do manage.

### Keep exits and corridors clear

FRNSW's advice not to store or charge devices near exits matters even more in a shared building. E-bikes and scooters parked in corridors, stairwells or lobbies can block the way out and add fuel to a fire in exactly the wrong place. Check common areas regularly, and remind residents where devices can and can't be left.

![Technician testing an illuminated green exit sign](svc9 "Clear exits and working exit signs help everyone get out quickly")

### Think about where charging happens

Basements, bike rooms and storage cages are common charging spots, often with nobody watching. Review whether power outlets in these areas are being used for charging, and talk to your strata committee or owners about house rules. Get advice before making changes, because any by-law needs to go through the proper strata process.

### Make sure detection and warning systems work

FRNSW recommends a working smoke alarm on every level of a home as a minimum. In apartment buildings, the smoke alarms in units, the detection in common areas and the building occupant warning system all need to work if a fire starts. Our guide to [smoke alarm rules for NSW strata apartments](/blog/smoke-alarm-rules-nsw-strata-apartments/) explains who is responsible for what, and our [smoke alarm testing](/smoke-alarm-testing/) service can help.

### Keep up routine maintenance

Your building's essential fire safety measures, such as detection, sprinklers, hydrants, exit signs and emergency lighting, are what protect residents if a battery fire does break out. Regular [routine fire inspections](/routine-fire-inspections/) to AS1851 help make sure those systems are ready, and our photographic reports show you exactly what needs attention.

### Share the message with residents

Many people simply don't know the risks. A short notice in the lift, a strata newsletter item or a welcome pack page with FRNSW's charging tips can make a real difference.
