Summer is here, and for many NSW buildings near bushland or grassland, that means bushfire risk. Most bushfire advice is written for people in freestanding homes, but strata committees, building managers and business owners have their own part to play. This guide covers **bushfire season preparation** for NSW strata and commercial buildings: what the official ratings and warnings mean, simple upkeep on common property, and why your building's own fire safety measures matter just as much in summer.

## When is bushfire season in NSW?

According to the NSW Rural Fire Service (RFS), the statutory Bush Fire Danger Period runs from **1 October to 31 March**, although it can vary with local conditions. During that period, you need a fire permit to burn in the open in Rural Fire districts. In Fire and Rescue NSW districts, a permit is needed at all times. Permits are free, and a total fire ban automatically suspends any permit you hold.

For building managers, the practical message is simple: don't plan any burning off of garden waste or landscaping debris on site without checking the permit rules first.

## Understanding fire danger ratings

NSW uses the Australian Fire Danger Rating System, which has four levels. The NSW Government sums up what to do at each one:

- **Moderate:** plan and prepare.
- **High:** be ready to act.
- **Extreme:** take action now to protect your life and property.
- **Catastrophic:** for your survival, leave bushfire-risk areas.

On Catastrophic days, the advice is to go to a safer location early in the morning or the night before. Safe Work Australia notes that ratings are published for the current day and three days ahead, and suggests workplaces check them in advance and again at the start of each day during the bushfire season.

## The three warning levels

Fire danger ratings tell you how dangerous a fire *would* be if one started. Warnings tell you about a fire that *has* started. Under the Australian Warning System there are three levels:

1. **Advice:** an incident has started and there's no immediate danger.
2. **Watch and Act:** there's a heightened level of threat.
3. **Emergency Warning:** the highest level of warning. Any delay puts lives at risk.

Safe Work Australia is clear that if a fire starts near you, you should act straight away and not wait for a warning. The NSW Government's [Hazards Near Me app](https://www.nsw.gov.au/emergency/hazards-near-me-app) is one way to keep track of warnings for the areas you look after.

## Bushfire season preparation on common property

The NSW Government's checklist for preparing a home for bushfire season translates well to strata common property and commercial sites. Items worth adding to your summer maintenance list include:

- **Clean gutters** of leaves and debris, and consider metal gutter guards.
- **Repair damaged or missing roof tiles.**
- **Fit seals around doors and windows** and repair or cover gaps in external walls, so embers have fewer ways in.
- **Keep lawns short and gardens maintained**, and prune plants that overhang buildings.
- **Clear away debris** such as leaves, sticks and firewood, and dispose of green waste regularly.
- **Store gas bottles** well away from the building, with the outlet pointing away.
- **Keep hoses long enough** to reach around the building.

In a strata scheme, much of this sits with the owners corporation as part of maintaining common property. A pre-summer walk around the grounds is an easy way to catch overhanging branches, blocked gutters and stored rubbish.

## Check your building's own fire safety measures

Bushfire season is also a good reminder that your building's essential fire safety measures need to be ready all year round. A fire inside the building can start on any day, and hot, dry, windy weather makes everything harder. Before summer, it's worth confirming that:

- **Routine servicing is up to date.** Your fire hydrants, hose reels, sprinklers, fire pumps, detection and alarm systems should be tested on schedule under AS1851. If you're unsure, check your last [routine inspection](/routine-fire-inspections/) report.
- **Defects have been fixed.** Any outstanding items on your defect report, such as leaking hose reels, faulty fire doors or failed emergency lights, should be repaired. Our team handles [fire equipment repairs](/fire-equipment-repairs/) across NSW.
- **Exits and paths of travel are clear.** Stairways, corridors and exit doors must stay free of stored items so people can get out quickly.
- **Fire doors close properly.** Fire doors that are wedged open or don't self-close can let smoke and fire spread through the building.
- **Hydrant boosters and fire service access are unobstructed.** Fire crews need to reach and connect to your booster without moving cars, bins or landscaping out of the way.
- **Your AFSS is current.** Your [annual fire safety statement](/afss-inspections/) confirms that each measure on your fire safety schedule has been assessed by a competent fire safety practitioner.

## Help residents and tenants make a plan

The RFS encourages everyone at risk to make a **bush fire survival plan**, a written plan for what to do if a fire threatens. It can be made online at myfireplan.com.au in a few minutes. The RFS breaks it into four steps: discuss what you'll do if a bushfire threatens, prepare your home and yourself, know the alert levels, and keep up to date with information sources.

For strata buildings, a short notice in the lobby or an email to residents early in summer can point people to these resources. It's also a good time to remind everyone not to store items in fire stairs or corridors, and to check that their smoke alarms are working.

For workplaces, Safe Work Australia notes that a person conducting a business or undertaking must eliminate or minimise risks so far as is reasonably practicable, and that includes bushfire risk. Its guidance suggests reviewing control measures on High days, reconsidering travel and relocating outdoor or remote workers on Extreme days, and postponing work or relocating workers where possible on Catastrophic days. If your workplace has an emergency plan, make sure it covers bushfire and that staff know what to do.

## A quick pre-summer checklist

1. Check whether your site is near bushland or grassland and how ratings will be monitored.
2. Clear gutters, prune overhanging plants and remove debris from common property.
3. Confirm routine fire safety servicing is up to date and defects are repaired.
4. Walk the exits, fire stairs and fire doors, and clear any stored items.
5. Make sure the hydrant booster and fire brigade access are unobstructed.
6. Share bushfire survival plan resources with residents, tenants or staff.
