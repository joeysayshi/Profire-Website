If you manage a strata building or commercial property in NSW, you've probably heard the term **AS1851** more often this year. Since 13 February 2026, AS 1851-2012 has been the mandatory standard for routine fire safety maintenance in most NSW buildings. But what does that routine maintenance actually involve, and what does it ask of building owners?

Here's a plain-English guide to the standard, what changed in 2026, and what you should have in place.

## What is AS 1851-2012?

AS 1851-2012 is the Australian Standard titled *Routine service of fire protection systems and equipment*. It sets out how fire protection systems should be serviced so they stay reliable for the life of the building. Routine servicing under the standard has four parts:

- **Inspection:** looking over equipment for damage, obstructions, missing parts or changes to the building that affect it
- **Testing:** operating systems and components to confirm they work as intended
- **Preventive maintenance:** the upkeep that stops faults developing
- **Survey:** checking that systems still suit the building as it's currently used

In practice, that means the systems listed on a building's Fire Safety Schedule, such as fire alarms, sprinklers, hydrants, pumps and extinguishers. The standard sets out the tasks and intervals for each type of system.

Standards Australia issued an amendment in November 2016 that corrected editorial errors and clarified the definition of "baseline data" (the reference information a system's performance is measured against).

## What changed for NSW buildings in 2026?

From 13 February 2026, maintaining essential fire safety measures to AS 1851-2012 became mandatory under the Environmental Planning and Assessment (Development Certification and Fire Safety) Regulation 2021.

According to the NSW Building Commission, the requirement applies to:

- all **class 1b and class 2 to class 9 buildings**, new and existing
- existing buildings even if they were approved and built before the change
- new buildings from the issue of the occupation certificate, which means developers must comply between completion and sale

The obligation sits with the building owner. In a strata scheme, that means the owners corporation and strata committee.

Importantly, AS 1851-2012 is about **servicing**, not upgrading. The Building Commission notes the standard doesn't require you to upgrade your systems.

## How often does AS1851 routine fire safety maintenance happen?

The standard sets out services at different intervals, from frequent routine checks through to yearly, five-yearly, ten-yearly and 25-yearly services. Which tasks fall due when depends on the type of system.

For existing buildings, the NSW Government has clarified the timing:

- The longer-interval services (5, 10 and 25-yearly) don't all have to be done straight away. Those intervals can be counted from 13 February 2026.
- The **yearly service is expected by February 2027**, whatever the building's age.

## Defects and non-conformances: what must be fixed and when?

Servicing will usually turn up some findings. The NSW Building Commission explains how the different kinds should be handled:

- **Critical defects** (where a system is inoperable) should be fixed as soon as possible, with interim measures in place to reduce the risk until they are.
- **Non-critical defects** should have a plan for rectification.
- **Non-conformances**, which are usually missing or incorrect information, should be assessed case by case with a suitably qualified person.

The Building Commission states that "the new legislation does not require non-conformance or non-critical defects to be resolved immediately." It also notes that missing baseline data is generally recorded as a non-critical defect or non-conformance rather than a critical defect.

## What records do you need to keep?

Record-keeping is a core part of the standard. Under the NSW rules:

- Records must be kept in the way AS 1851-2012 sets out, for **at least seven years**, and be available for inspection.
- The standard requires a **hard-copy logbook kept on site**. Electronic records don't yet replace it.
- If you change fire contractors, the existing logbook should be handed to the new contractor.

## Does AS1851 cover every fire safety measure?

Not quite. The requirement applies to the essential fire safety measures listed on your Fire Safety Schedule and recorded on your Annual Fire Safety Statement (AFSS). However, the Building Commission notes that some essential measures, such as emergency exit lighting, aren't covered by AS 1851-2012. Those still need to be maintained to the standard listed on your Fire Safety Schedule or the original design.

## How does AS1851 relate to your AFSS?

They're connected, but separate. Routine servicing under AS 1851-2012 happens throughout the year. The AFSS is the yearly statement you lodge with council and Fire and Rescue NSW, confirming each listed measure has been assessed by an accredited practitioner (fire safety) in the previous 12 months.

The Building Commission says AS 1851-2012 work doesn't have to be finished before you lodge your AFSS. If a critical defect might delay your statement, tell your council early. Our [AFSS inspections](/afss-inspections/) page explains how that process works.

## What happens if you don't comply?

Council is the regulator for both AS 1851-2012 compliance and AFSS review. The NSW Government warns of significant penalties, citing fines of $33,000 for individuals and $66,000 for corporations, and non-compliance can also lead to enforcement action or a fire safety order.

## A practical checklist for owners and strata managers

Based on the NSW Building Commission's guidance:

1. Check your AFSS (displayed in the building) or Fire Safety Schedule to confirm which measures are essential.
2. Engage a suitably qualified fire contractor, and check licences for any specialist trade work such as electrical or plumbing.
3. Make sure your maintenance contract follows the AS 1851-2012 schedules.
4. Keep the logbook on site and all fire safety records for at least seven years.
5. Plan how you'll rectify non-critical defects, and have non-conformances assessed.
6. Contact council early if your AFSS might be late.
