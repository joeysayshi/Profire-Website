Ask a room of strata owners **who is responsible for fire safety in a strata building** and you'll hear a few different answers: the strata manager, the committee, the fire contractor, maybe the council. Each of them has a part to play, but the legal responsibility sits in one place. This guide explains who carries the duty in NSW, who does the work, and what each party should be checking.

## The short answer: the owners corporation

In NSW, fire safety duties fall on the **building owner**. In a strata scheme, the owner of the common property is the owners corporation, and the owners corporation is made up of every lot owner in the scheme.

The Building Commission NSW puts it plainly: the owner is ultimately accountable for fire safety and for protecting the building's occupants. NSW Government guidance for strata committees adds that owners and the owners corporation can be held responsible, and fined, if the required maintenance standard isn't followed.

Two laws work together here:

- **Strata Schemes Management Act 2015.** The owners corporation is responsible for maintaining and repairing the common property. Section 106 requires it to keep common property in a state of good and serviceable repair, a duty the courts treat as strict.
- **Environmental Planning and Assessment (Development Certification and Fire Safety) Regulation 2021.** This sets out the building owner's duties for essential fire safety measures, such as fire alarms, sprinklers, hydrants, exit signs and fire doors, and for the annual fire safety statement (AFSS).

## What the owners corporation must do

The Building Commission's guidance for building owners, including owners corporations and strata committees, lists the core obligations:

- **Maintain the measures to AS 1851-2012.** Since 13 February 2026, essential fire safety measures must be inspected and tested in line with AS 1851-2012 (or another standard set by a performance solution for your building).
- **Use competent people.** Maintenance must be carried out by competent persons, and some follow-up work needs a specialist licence, such as electrical or plumbing.
- **Keep systems working.** Systems must be operational at all times, and identified defects should be fixed promptly. Non-critical defects don't have to be fixed immediately, but they shouldn't be ignored.
- **Keep records.** Records must be kept as AS 1851-2012 prescribes, for at least seven years, and be available for inspection.
- **Keep a logbook on site.** A hard-copy logbook must stay in the building. Electronic records alone aren't enough. If you change contractors, the logbook goes to the new one.
- **Issue the AFSS.** Each year, the owner issues an AFSS confirming every measure on the fire safety schedule has been assessed in the past 12 months. Only an accredited practitioner (fire safety) can do that assessment.

The AFSS is lodged with the local council and Fire and Rescue NSW (FRNSW), and council guidance notes that a copy of the statement and the fire safety schedule must be displayed prominently in the building.

The guidance also makes a point that often reassures committees: AS 1851-2012 is about servicing, not upgrading. Existing systems don't have to be brought up to current standards unless the council orders it.

## Who does what in practice

Responsibility and day-to-day work are two different things. Here's how the roles usually split.

### The strata committee

The committee acts for the owners corporation between general meetings. NSW Government guidance suggests committees ask their contractor a simple question: are you carrying out maintenance in accordance with AS 1851? The committee also approves quotes for defect repairs and keeps fire safety on the agenda.

### The strata manager

An owners corporation can delegate functions to a strata managing agent, but only if a general meeting resolves to do so (section 52 of the Strata Schemes Management Act). Many schemes hand the strata manager the job of organising the AFSS and the contractors. Delegating the paperwork doesn't move the legal duty, though. The owners corporation remains the building owner.

### The fire contractor

The contractor inspects, tests and services the equipment to AS 1851-2012, records the work in the on-site logbook and reports defects. You don't need to be an accredited practitioner to do routine maintenance work, but the Building Commission recommends checking a contractor's licensing and asking for written recommendations that cite the relevant clause or standard.

### The accredited practitioner (fire safety)

This is the person who assesses each measure and endorses the AFSS. It's a separate role from routine servicing, and it requires accreditation. Our post on [Level 1 vs Level 2 fire safety measures](/blog/level-1-vs-level-2-fire-safety-measures/) explains how that accreditation works.

### Lot owners and tenants

Individual owners are part of the owners corporation, so they share in its responsibility. Inside their own apartment, they also have their own duties, the most common being smoke alarms. If the lot is rented, landlords have extra obligations under the tenancy laws. Our guide to [smoke alarm rules for NSW strata apartments](/blog/smoke-alarm-rules-nsw-strata-apartments/) covers those in detail.

### Council and Fire and Rescue NSW

Local councils are the regulator for AS 1851-2012 maintenance and receive the AFSS. FRNSW also receives a copy of every AFSS. Where things go wrong, councils can take enforcement action, including fire safety orders.

## What happens if it isn't done?

The Building Commission notes that penalties are set out in sections 81 and 81A of the Development Certification and Fire Safety Regulation, with fines of $33,000 for individuals and $66,000 for corporations. Councils can also issue fire safety orders.

Beyond the fines, the bigger risk is equipment that doesn't work when it's needed. That's why the regulations focus on regular servicing, written records and prompt repairs.

## A quick checklist for strata committees

1. **Find your fire safety schedule.** It lists the measures your building must maintain and the standard for each.
2. **Confirm your contractor services to AS 1851-2012** and fills in the on-site logbook at every visit.
3. **Check who organises the AFSS** and when it's due, and diarise it.
4. **Make sure an accredited practitioner** is assessing the measures for the AFSS.
5. **Keep seven years of records** and make sure they can be found quickly.
6. **Track defects** until they're fixed, starting with anything critical.
7. **Confirm any delegation to your strata manager** was approved at a general meeting.
