If your building has essential fire safety measures, the owner has to issue an annual fire safety statement (AFSS) every year. So what happens if your AFSS is late in NSW? In short, it becomes an offence the moment the due date passes, and councils can issue penalty notices that climb every week the statement stays outstanding. This guide explains how that works and how strata managers, building owners and facility managers can stay ahead of it.

## What an AFSS is and who it goes to

An annual fire safety statement is issued by or on behalf of the owner of an existing building. It covers every essential fire safety measure listed on the building's fire safety schedule, such as fire detection and alarm systems, hydrants, sprinklers, exit signs and emergency lighting, and fire doors. It also confirms the building's exit systems comply with the Regulation. NSW Planning publishes a standard template for it.

The requirements sit in the *Environmental Planning and Assessment (Development Certification and Fire Safety) Regulation 2021*. Once the statement is issued, the owner needs to:

- give it to the local council
- give a copy to the Commissioner of Fire and Rescue NSW, which accepts statements through an online form
- display a copy in a prominent position in the building

Each measure must be assessed by an accredited practitioner (fire safety). Councils such as Penrith point out that those assessments have to be carried out within the three months before the date the statement is issued, so the inspection can't be done too early either.

## When is your AFSS due?

There's no single state-wide date. Each building has its own cycle, and a new statement is due within 12 months of the last one (or of the fire safety certificate that started the cycle). Your council's records set the date, and many councils send reminder letters. Don't rely on those alone, though. Burwood Council, for example, says its reminders are a courtesy and the legal responsibility to submit on time stays with the owner.

## What happens if your AFSS is late

Failing to give the council an annual fire safety statement on time is an offence under the Regulation. Councils enforce it with penalty notices, and the amounts rise for each week the statement is overdue. Wollongong, Burwood and Hornsby councils all publish the same scale:

- first week late: **$1,000**
- second week late: **$2,000**
- third week late: **$3,000**
- fourth and every later week: **$4,000**

Councils treat each week as a separate offence, so these notices can stack up quickly while a statement stays overdue. Paying a fine doesn't make the obligation go away either. Hornsby Council notes that the AFSS still has to be submitted after a penalty notice has been issued.

Councils also warn that the matter can go further. Wollongong and Burwood both say an overdue statement can lead to proceedings in the Land and Environment Court, where much higher maximum penalties apply than for a penalty notice. Some councils also charge an administration fee when they issue an overdue notice.

The amounts above are what councils were publishing at the time of writing and can change, so check your own council's fire safety page for the current figures.

## Can you get an extension?

Sometimes. Several councils let owners apply for more time when something genuinely unexpected stops them lodging on time. A few things to know:

- **Ask early and explain why.** Penrith asks for a request by email that gives the reason and the date the statement will be completed.
- **It isn't guaranteed.** Penrith says extensions are unlikely where a building has a history of poor compliance or development work is proposed.
- **There may be a fee.** Hornsby charges an administration fee set in its fees and charges.
- **Your cycle doesn't move.** Burwood notes that an extension doesn't change the AFSS due date or the testing schedule for future years.

## Common reasons statements run late

Most late statements aren't caused by owners ignoring the rules. They usually come down to timing. A few things that tend to cause delays:

- **Booking the inspection too close to the due date.** If the assessment turns up a defect, there may not be time to fix it and re-check it before the deadline.
- **Defects that need parts or trades.** Repairs to items like fire doors, pumps or detection systems can take longer than expected.
- **Access problems.** Measures inside apartments or locked plant rooms can hold up the whole building.
- **Changes in strata or building management.** The due date can get lost when a building changes managers or owners.

## How to keep your AFSS on time

A little planning goes a long way:

1. **Know your due date.** Check your last statement or ask your council, and put it in your calendar with a reminder about four months out.
2. **Book the assessment early in the three-month window.** That leaves room to repair defects before the statement has to be issued.
3. **Keep up with routine maintenance.** Buildings that have regular servicing to AS1851 throughout the year are less likely to have surprises at AFSS time. See our [routine fire inspections](/routine-fire-inspections/) page.
4. **Arrange access in advance.** Give residents and tenants notice so technicians can reach every measure on the schedule.
5. **Lodge all three copies.** Council, Fire and Rescue NSW, and the copy displayed in the building.
